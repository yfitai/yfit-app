# YFIT video drafts: show one useful task, then invite a small next step

**Prepared for Don Campbell — November 27, 2026. These are written drafts, not rendered videos or approved public posts.**

Keep the current tracker, reporting workflow, prices and offers. Verify the customer analytics dashboard and the actual one-month trial behavior rather than inventing a replacement. Keep Go unchanged for the first creative comparison too: if both the video and destination change, we cannot tell which change helped. Native watch-time learning can begin after approval while support is still resolving Go reporting, but website conversion remains unknown until measured properly.

The first job is not to persuade someone to understand the whole app. It is to show one useful thing, make the next step clear, and let the person judge the sample experience before creating an account.

## Draft A: “Where are my workout notes?”

**Purpose:** demonstrate a specific, ordinary workout-organization task. Suggested first draft length: about 20–25 seconds, adjusted after timing the recorded narration. No logo animation or unrelated stock montage at the beginning.

**Full narration draft:**

> Still hunting through notes for your last workout? Here’s a sample workout in YFIT. Open Fitness, choose History, and tap the workout to see its exercises, weights and reps. Want to look around first? Use the profile link and choose Explore the app first. These are examples; your own saved workouts need an account.

**Opening:** Start on a legible workout screen while saying the question. Use a small “Sample data” label. The screen should fill enough of the vertical video to make the relevant details readable.

**App action:** In the existing sample app, open **Fitness → History**, then expand one workout card. The current guest Fitness component actually supports the History tab and expandable exercise/weight/repetition rows. Hold the useful rows on screen instead of rapidly cycling through five features. These are preloaded examples, not a newly generated plan or proof of a training outcome.

**Ending:** Show the current Go page’s **Explore the app first** button. Use the profile-link wording on Instagram or YouTube. On Pinterest, adapt the spoken instruction to its direct Visit site destination. Do not advertise a caption URL as clickable where it is not.

**Production boundary:** Do not show “Log a similar workout” as if it saves without an account. In the guest code, logging or starting a workout opens the signup prompt. Some guest cards also contain embedded phone mockups; frame the actual interactive history/details area, not an illustrated phone presented as a live private-account screen. Avoid the guest page’s unverified medication-adjustment copy in this demonstration.

## Draft B: “A weekly medication isn’t an everyday task”

**Purpose:** demonstrate YFIT’s weekday scheduling, a concrete differentiator already included in Build 109. This is an organizational demonstration, not advice about what medicine, dose or schedule someone should use. Suggested draft length: about 20–25 seconds.

**Full narration draft:**

> A weekly medication isn’t an everyday task. In this YFIT example, I choose once weekly and select Monday. The schedule shows the day I chose. Want to see how YFIT organizes medications alongside your fitness routine? Open the sample app from the profile link. Browse first; your own records require an account and the appropriate plan.

**Opening:** Show the frequency/day selection, not stock footage of pills or an unsupported interaction warning. Label the entry **Demo Medication — fictional example**. Avoid real account or health records.

**App action:** In an isolated sample-data preview of the actual authenticated Medications component, show the frequency selector, select **once weekly**, choose **Monday**, and show the resulting sample schedule. The weekday feature is backed by the earlier Build 109 implementation and owner device confirmation; the precise recording still needs to be prepared and checked. Any save used to produce the shot must stay in the local sample fixture, not create or change a production medical record.

**Ending:** Invite exploration of the sample experience. Do not claim that the current public guest medication page has this identical editing flow. Guest medication add/log/report controls currently open signup; the public preview and authenticated weekday editor are different experiences.

**Production boundary:** Do not claim medication compatibility, safe dosing, injury prevention, automatic treatment decisions or pain relief. This draft demonstrates choosing the intended day for an already prescribed schedule. Do not imply that every medication feature is permanently free: the app’s account/plan restrictions still apply.

## Draft C: a simple question-and-answer video

**Question on screen:** “Do I need an account just to look?” Suggested draft length: about 15–25 seconds, depending on the recording pace.

**Full narration draft:**

> Do I need an account just to look at YFIT? Not for the sample app. Tap Explore the app first, then look through the example screens. When you want your own saved records, you’ll need an account; Google or email are options. Start with a look around through the profile link.

**App action:** Show the current **Go → Explore the app first → guest dashboard → Fitness** journey. If useful, show **Start Workout** opening the existing signup modal, then close it without choosing Google or submitting a form. The source supports that prompt and dismissal. The modal offers **Continue with Google** and an email alternative. Do not complete sign-in merely to film the draft.

The existing route `https://app.yfitai.com/go?mode=guest` is a sample-data app entry, not a free Pro account. A future direct-demo destination could remove a step, but changing social links, preserving source tags and checking the live guest experience would be a separate approved test. For the first comparison, leave the existing destination unchanged.

## Which draft to start with

Start with **A** if we want a broad, low-risk organization benefit. Keep **B** as a distinctive medication-scheduling concept and **C** as an answer to registration hesitation. They should not all be mixed into one 30-second video.

For the cleanest first comparison, make two versions of the same topic: the current rights-cleared explanatory format and a focused app-action format. Keep the destination, offer and approximate length the same. Comparing a workout topic with a medication topic simultaneously changes both message and format; that is a useful exploration later, but it does not isolate a video-style effect.

Use several relevant cuts if needed. The change is **one story**, not a rule that the entire video must be a single static clip. A screen recording is preferable for proving a sequence; the four restored PNGs can support an early visual draft, but still pictures alone do not demonstrate a tap or a changing schedule.

## Does this require n8n changes?

**Not to prepare these written scripts or offline video drafts.** No live workflow edit, execution or extra public post is required for preparation.

For regular automated publishing, there will probably be a small change somewhere in the content-generation or video-input/template stage. The existing scraper can remain a source of topic ideas. Instead of converting an article into five unrelated scenes, the selected format would produce a hook, one app workflow and a close. Its visual input would include an approved app recording or relevant screenshots, rather than assuming the current montage already supports interactive demonstration.

Whether the change belongs in the Supabase content generator, the video service’s request/template, or an n8n field depends on the actual current request contract. That live workflow inspection remains unavailable through the saved API access, so do not promise “one n8n setting” or replace the workflow from an old export. Prefer retaining the existing data contract and upload nodes wherever possible. No such change has been made or is authorized by this draft sheet.

Keep the existing posting schedule, six-platform upload/account connections and current report workflow. If the owner approves public testing later, use selected normal posting slots; do not add extra executions, retries or backfills. A manually scheduled single offline-produced draft could be a separate pilot option if automation changes would be disproportionate, but it still needs approval of the actual video, destination and publishing action.

## Plain-language order of work

1. **Now:** review these scripts, choose the first concept, and prepare an offline sample video after approval. Continue waiting for support’s existing-tracker dashboard mapping. No page, offer or workflow change is needed for reviewing drafts.
2. **First public test, after approval:** vary only the video format within normal posting slots. Compare native viewing/retention at matching post ages. Keep Go, offers and links constant. Until Go reporting is trustworthy, do not call a watch-time improvement a conversion improvement.
3. **Then:** if measured arrivals reach Go but few start the sample app, test one clearer first-screen message or focused demo entry. Do not delete the full information page just because it is long.
4. **Last:** consider founder help or a bounded free-Pro preview only if people can reach useful value, trial entitlement/expiry are verified, and the existing one-month offer is not contradicted.

The actual app, accounts, prices, trial configuration, n8n workflow, posting/report schedules, analytics tracker and phone Build 109 remain unchanged. This document is a draft for discussion—not approval to implement, generate paid media, publish or alter a customer’s records.
