## Change the story, not simply the number of clips

A generic montage may look commercial when it combines stock footage, a sensational article headline, broad product claims, and no visible connection to a useful task. That is a plausible concern, not an independently observed description of YFIT’s complete current videos. The better question is whether a viewer understands the problem, sees credible product operation, and knows the next small step.

TikTok’s small-business creative playbook recommends a hook, explanation, and close, with early problem-led openings, natural creator voice, captions, and product explanation. Meta likewise recommends an early reason to stop, native-feeling presentation, sound-off captions, and a clear CTA.[1] [2] These are principally paid-ad/platform recommendations. They justify an organic experiment, not a promised YFIT lift.

Start with roughly 20–30 seconds, introducing the job or app action in the first 3–6 seconds. Those ranges are working hypotheses, not an ideal length established for this audience. A 12–15-second cutdown can come later if the longer demonstration is understandable but loses attention. Avoid testing length, audience, offer, destination, and visual style simultaneously.

A coherent story can have a founder opening, a screen recording, a close-up of the relevant result, and a founder close. Those four scenes all answer the same question. One continuous shot is optional; five unrelated clips are the problem hypothesis. Focused Q&A is especially useful when the question is practical: “Can I look before creating an account?” or “Where do I find the sample workout?” General “What is YFIT?” answers can drift back into a catalogue.

The initial audience hypothesis is a recreational exerciser with a fragmented recurring routine who wants to see the next workout step and review what is already recorded. This targets an ordinary task, not a diagnosis. Medication-and-fitness organization can later be demonstrated as a privacy-conscious workflow differentiator, but do not recruit by prescriptions or claim interaction safety. Test form-review interest separately after the routine concept, rather than mixing two jobs into the first comparison.

Articles should supply research leads, not copied hooks or borrowed credibility. Verify date, scope, and the underlying fact; paraphrase originally and retain attribution. Attribution alone does not license article screenshots, distinctive text, photos, clips, or music, and there is no fixed number of seconds or words that automatically makes reuse fair.[19] YouTube’s originality rules are also distinct from copyright permission; licensed material can still be insufficiently original for monetization.[20]

Before a confident creative autopsy, obtain two or three actual posted videos with their captions, CTA/link destinations, and original publication dates. Review the full videos, opening frames, readable app screens, sound, pacing, and ending. Pair each with native retention or watch-time screenshots/exports at comparable post ages, using the same metric definitions. Without that pairing, claims about “commercial style” causing first-second exits remain speculation.

The brief’s latest November 27 update supersedes the earlier asset-availability concern: four owner-approved fictional-data PNGs were uploaded and verified publicly accessible. Seven other catalogue assets remain unverified. Screenshot insertion and a screenshot-free fallback exist, so corrected assets are not proof of successful future overlays. Inspect the rendered output during the normal approved process before publication; do not restart, retry, backfill, or add an extra run. Screenshots show interface appearance; a screen recording better demonstrates an actual sequence.

## Four scripts to prepare for filming

These are illustrative production briefs, not customer testimonials or medical advice. Use real founder/creator speech, captions, and clearly labelled demo data. The actions below use verified guest routes; any deeper button interaction must be checked against the approved current interface before filming. Do not portray sample screens as newly generated AI results. No proposed feature-specific URL should be published until implemented and approved.

### 1. Routine demo: “What’s my next workout step?” — 22–26 seconds

**0–3 seconds:** Start on the app, not a logo animation. Voice: “Your workout shouldn’t start with hunting for your notes.” Keep a small, legible “Sample data” label on the demonstration.

**3–8 seconds:** Open the existing guest experience, then its Fitness view. Voice: “This is YFIT’s sample workout view.” Show the real navigation action clearly.

**8–18 seconds:** Hold on the available sample exercise or workout details long enough to read them, then return to the dashboard. Voice: “Your workout details here. Your overview here. See the layout before you sign up.” Production note: do not invent a plan-editing action or claim a training result; show only actions the approved demo supports.

**18–26 seconds:** “Want to see whether this layout fits your routine? Open the sample workout through the profile link. Browse first; an account is needed for your own saved records.” The destination remains the existing Go/guest journey, with honest navigation instructions until focused entry exists.

### 2. Founder Q&A: “Do I have to sign up just to look?” — 24–28 seconds

**0–4 seconds:** Off-camera question: “Do I need an account just to see YFIT?” Founder: “Not for the sample-data demo. Let me show you.”

**4–12 seconds:** Show Go’s actual “Explore the app first” button, enter guest mode, and navigate to Fitness. Keep the guest banner visible. Voice: “These are examples, not your personal records.”

**12–21 seconds:** Show an existing gated record/save action and the contextual signup modal, only after confirming the actual trigger. Voice: “When you want your own records, there’s an account step. Google or email are options; you can also keep exploring.” Do not imply that signup automatically migrates guest work.

**21–28 seconds:** “Open the guest demo from the profile link and look around before deciding.” This answers account friction directly without advertising Google as no signup.

### 3. One-feature nutrition preview: “Can I see the food-log layout first?” — 20–24 seconds

**0–3 seconds:** Founder asks: “Want to see a food log before entering anything personal?” Immediately show the guest Nutrition view.

**3–15 seconds:** Navigate from the dashboard to Nutrition and show the existing fictional entries and displayed summary. Voice: “Here’s the sample log and how it is presented. You can inspect it without sharing your own meals.” Do not imply the sample was just scanned, saved, or clinically personalized.

**15–24 seconds:** “Browse the nutrition example through the profile link. Your own saved tracking needs an account.” Keep this as a later job hypothesis, not a simultaneous first-round audience test.

### 4. Form-review preview: “What does this screen actually show?” — 24–30 seconds

**0–4 seconds:** Begin on the form-analysis interface. Voice: “What would a form-review screen actually show you?”

**4–18 seconds:** Open the existing guest form-analysis route and point out only the visible sample information. Voice: “This is a sample walkthrough of the interface—not a live assessment of you.” If a later approved recording shows an actual camera session, label permissions and inputs accurately rather than substituting it silently.

**18–24 seconds:** “Look through the example, then decide whether you want to explore more.” Production note: avoid framing a score as proof of safety or claiming injury prevention, pain relief or diagnosis. Keep necessary sample/privacy information clear without making the spoken ad a string of disclaimers.

**24–30 seconds:** “Open the sample form-review screen through the profile link before deciding whether to create an account.” Do not promise unrestricted camera analysis without signup.

For YouTube, “profile link” means a channel profile link; Shorts-description and comment URLs are non-clickable. A clickable Related Video can also continue the explanation, but is another video step, not a direct website click.[15] Instagram’s profile supports public links; say “link in profile,” not “click the caption URL.”[16] Pinterest can later use a direct Pin destination and its outbound-click measure.[17] TikTok should use an on-platform CTA until website eligibility is confirmed; official eligibility is at least 1,000 followers or a Verified Business Account.[18] These recommendations do not authorize account connections or profile changes.

## Measure the handoffs without inventing an all-platform conversion rate

Use the sequence **native attention → profile/outbound action → verified Go arrival → first use → first valuable action → signup → returning useful action**. Keep each handoff’s denominator separate. YouTube Shorts distinguishes stayed-to-watch, engaged views, and average percentage viewed; Instagram distinguishes views, estimated viewers, and watch time.[13] [14] These are platform measures, not interchangeable counts of unique people.

Each reported number needs its source, capture date, start/end dates, timezone, host/path where relevant, and counting grain: post, account, play, estimated viewer, click, session, event, or authenticated user. Compare posts at equivalent ages. Separate paid from organic, customers from prospects, and owner/staff testing from acquisition where existing data permits. If historical testing cannot be separated, mark that uncertainty rather than subtracting a guess.

A qualified Go arrival should be a recorded eligible acquisition session on the canonical host and exact path, after applicable consent, with known testing/customer status where possible. It is not a verified unique person. Report unobserved or consent-excluded traffic honestly. Referrers and UTM campaign tags are separate fields; a referrer does not establish campaign attribution. Use only an owner-agreed safe tag allowlist and the existing tracker identity, with no health details in URLs or analytics payloads.

For the first routine test, propose first use as opening the sample Fitness view. Proposed activation is completing its intended review task and reaching the relevant overview/result, using an existing observable event or consented manual observation. A page opening alone may be weak evidence of value. If current instrumentation cannot distinguish the task, activation remains unknown until an approved solution exists. Do not invent events retrospectively.

Signup completion means an authenticated account outcome, not merely clicking Google or submitting an email form. Seven-day and fourteen-day return should mean a defined useful action by an eligible cohort with enough elapsed follow-up. Account return can be deduplicated by user ID; local guest return is only browser/session evidence. A repeat video play is not product retention. Never divide total signups by summed native views to claim an end-to-end, all-platform unique conversion rate. Mark missing data as missing, not zero.

2. **Creative phase:** After approval, use a 14–28-day window within existing posting slots. A native-attention-only comparison can begin while support resolves dashboard access, provided downstream Go conversion stays explicitly unknown. Do not call improved watch time a signup or retention improvement. Compare two formats for the same routine job: an original, rights-cleared montage-style control and the focused demo in Script 1. Keep destination, offer, duration range, voice, CTA, and practical posting conditions stable. Alternate formats in balanced order on YouTube and Instagram; do not treat one platform as the other’s control. The other scripts are a preparation library, not simultaneous experimental arms. Capture native attention at a predeclared comparable age, for example 72 hours; observe downstream handoffs only when valid measurement is available.

3. **First-experience phase:** Advance only when handoffs and Go arrivals are interpretable. Hold the chosen creative and offer constant; compare current Go with one owner-approved focused entry over another proposed 14–28-day window. Use balanced time blocks if safe concurrent assignment is unavailable, acknowledging timing effects. Measure demo starts and first valuable actions before emphasizing signup. If arrivals are unknown, repair access/measurement first. If a verified eligible count is zero, investigate CTA, link availability, and handoff integrity; there are no arrivals from which to diagnose landing abandonment.

4. **Offer phase:** Proceed only if people can reach measurable value. First investigate why activated people do or do not return. If entitlement remains a plausible obstacle, run one bounded founder cohort against the existing free/offer path, with consistent eligibility and reconciled terms. Enrollment can use a proposed 14–28-day window; preview duration and follow-up are separate clocks. Judge useful return, free continuation, explicit paid selection, cost, support burden, and complaints—not trial-to-paid percentage alone.

These windows organize work; they do not guarantee adequate samples. With dozens of views and perhaps very few arrivals, a single action can change the apparent ranking. Organic distribution and sequential blocks are not randomized causal proof. Report absolute counts alongside any properly matched rates, extend observation or remain inconclusive when needed, and avoid universal “good” conversion rates or invented lift thresholds.

A reasonable directional decision is to continue a concept that repeatedly makes the job clearer, improves comparable native attention, and produces credible downstream exploration without misleading claims. Better watch time with no measured handoff is not landing success. More signups with no useful return is not durable activation. If evidence stays sparse, use interviews to identify the next hypothesis rather than declaring a statistical winner.

## Use human feedback without borrowing health credibility

With separate approval, invite a few consenting viewers or known users to voluntary interviews or manual onboarding. Ask what they expected from the clip, where they thought the link would lead, what they understood from the sample, and what stopped their next action. Let them explain the job back in their own words. Use fictional data, obtain permission before recording, avoid unnecessary health details, and do not recruit by diagnosis, medication, injury, or other sensitive attributes.

Offer an optional founder-guided walkthrough rather than unsolicited pressure to register. Record assisted use separately from self-serve use; helpful human support does not prove the landing page works unaided. Explain incentives or founder connections and do not turn interview comments into testimonials without explicit consent and truthful context.[22]

Before eventual publication, check claim support, asset rights, consent, sample labels, and applicable AI disclosures. FTC guidance covers implied as well as explicit health claims; a disclaimer cannot rescue an unsupported pain, injury, medication, or safety promise.[21] Realistic synthetic or materially altered media may require YouTube, TikTok, or Meta labels.[23] [24] [25] Labels do not make fabricated customers or clinical endorsements acceptable. The credible proof YFIT can show now is what its interface does—not an unmeasured health outcome.
