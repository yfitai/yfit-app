# Audience focus, platform CTAs, and a low-traffic test plan

## Recommendation: one job, two priority platforms

YFIT should not treat the current six-platform output as six equivalent broadcasts. The native evidence in the brief is small and differently scoped: the November 17 YouTube Short had 71–72 views and 26.6% average percentage viewed in its first 10 hours 58 minutes; the channel had 474 views and 4 subscribers over 28 days. Instagram showed 110 views, 107 viewers, 8 interactions, and 1 account engaged over 30 days; its latest Reel had 18 views, 17 viewers, and 1 interaction. Facebook showed 498 views, 70 three-second views, 6 comments, and 0 follows in a different October 18–November 16 window. Pinterest showed 2 impressions, 2 Pin clicks, and 0 outbound clicks in the displayed context. TikTok showed 22 cumulative likes, 0 followers, and no verified Website field. These are directional starting observations, not comparable rates, and do not establish a first-second drop-off, no Go traffic, or marketing failure. Do not use a bogus combined “70k report total.”

The narrowest useful audience hypothesis is: **a person who already exercises, takes one or more regular medications or manages a recurring routine, and wants a low-friction way to organize what to do before/after a workout without turning health information into a public social post.** The job to be done is: “When my routine is fragmented, help me capture a simple plan and see the next action quickly, so I can follow through and review what I did.” This is a product-organization hypothesis, not health targeting. Copy must not infer a viewer’s diagnosis, promise medication outcomes, or suggest that YFIT replaces clinical advice.

A second, safer acquisition hypothesis should be tested separately: **a recreational exerciser who wants to understand whether a movement is being recorded consistently, and will try a form-check demonstration if the limits are shown honestly.** The job is: “When I am unsure what I am doing, show me what the app records and what it does not judge, so I can decide whether the workflow is useful.” Demonstrate the interface with fictional data or a consenting demonstrator. Say “form cues” or “movement review,” not “prevent injury,” “fix pain,” or a guaranteed safety result.

Prioritize **YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels** for the first creative test. YouTube supplies a native Shorts “stayed to watch” measure and average percentage viewed; its official definition says engaged views are viewers who stayed past the initial seconds, while average percentage viewed is calculated from engaged views and watch time [1]. Instagram supplies viewers (estimated unique accounts), views, watch time, average watch time, and follows; Instagram explicitly distinguishes viewers from views, which can include repeat plays [2]. This makes the two platforms useful for comparing the same hook and demonstration, while keeping each platform’s native definitions separate. Pinterest can be a later destination test because Pins support an explicit destination URL and an official outbound-click metric [3]. TikTok should be treated as awareness and message learning only until a link is available: TikTok’s official rule says a profile website link requires at least 1,000 followers or a Verified Business Account [4].

## Platform CTA and link rules

On YouTube, do not tell Short viewers to click a URL placed in the Short description or comments. YouTube says those URLs are non-clickable; it does allow channel profile links and a clickable Related Video link on a Short [5]. The proposed CTA is therefore: “See the 20-second workflow on the channel profile,” with one direct YFIT profile link, or “Open the related demo.” If a long-form explainer is later made, its description can be a separate CTA surface subject to YouTube’s eligibility rules. Measure channel-profile visits or related-video continuation where available, not imagined Short-description clicks.

On Instagram, use one direct profile link to the intended Go/demo destination and say “Link in profile.” Instagram’s documentation supports adding a website to a profile [6] and states that profile links are public profile information [7]. Do not assume a caption URL is clickable or count a typed URL as a visit. A Reel can instead ask viewers to visit the profile; record Instagram profile activity and the tagged destination’s verified arrivals separately. Keep the landing URL stable during a creative comparison so a broken or changed profile link does not become a creative verdict.

On Pinterest, publish a Pin with a direct destination URL, preferably a single YFIT Go/demo page rather than a link chain. Pinterest defines outbound clicks as clicks to the destination URL associated with the Pin or ad [3]. Use Pinterest’s own outbound-click count as the platform handoff metric, then compare it with tagged Go arrivals. The current 2-impression/0-outbound-click observation is too small to diagnose anything.

On TikTok, until the website field is eligible, use an on-platform CTA such as “follow for the next workflow” or “search YFIT,” and do not label views as site acquisition. If eligibility changes, use a direct website link rather than a multi-hop link chain; TikTok warns that in-app browsing may limit external link chains [4]. Any product-promoting post should follow TikTok’s disclosure requirements, which should be checked before publication.

Source tags are proposed only on an allowlist agreed in advance: one stable host, exact `/go` path, and a small set of safe values such as `utm_source=youtube|instagram|pinterest|tiktok`, `utm_medium=organic_social`, and a creative code. Do not add new trackers, properties, credentials, or analytics systems. A source tag is not proof of a person, and a referrer is not equivalent to a UTM source.

## Staged, controlled organic test

**Stage 0 — measurement readiness (before posting).** Confirm that the existing owning-project dashboard can show the exact `/go` path, selected date range, consented analytics behavior, referrer, and agreed allowlist tags. Do not browse the live app as a test, sign up, or change production settings. Define the funnel as attention → profile or outbound click → qualified Go arrival → first use → activation → signup → returning use. Proposed activation is a meaningful in-app action such as completing one demo workflow or saving a non-sensitive sample; the owner must confirm the actual event definition before use. Because verified Go counts are unresolved, no landing-page or offer diagnosis is valid yet.

**Stage 1 — creative only.** Make two tightly bounded concepts, not five unrelated clips: (A) one focused app demonstration for “organize the next workout step,” and (B) one form-check demonstration showing the input, the recorded output, and a plain-language limitation. Keep duration, voice, aspect ratio, and CTA placement as similar as practical. Use the same landing destination and honest beta wording for all variants. Post in a balanced order, for example A on YouTube, B on Instagram, then B on YouTube and A on Instagram on the next comparable posting window. Do not post all variants simultaneously or infer causality from platform differences. Read native attention measures after a predeclared window, such as 72 hours, but treat small counts as directional. Compare stayed-to-watch, average percentage viewed, Instagram viewers versus views, watch time, profile activity, comments that indicate the job, and any verified downstream arrival. Views, likes, and comments alone are not success.

A practical proposed decision rule is qualitative: advance the concept that shows a clearer job signal and less early abandonment across both platforms, with at least one downstream handoff, rather than declaring a statistically significant winner. The thresholds are hypotheses for this account, not industry benchmarks; with dozens of views, uncertainty is too large for spurious significance claims.

**Stage 2 — landing experience.** Hold the winning concept and offer constant, then compare the current Go entry with one shorter, focused first experience: a visible sample workflow before asking for an account, with save/record, personal health data, paid AI, abuse controls, and identity actions gated as already designed. The test question is whether qualified arrivals reach first use, not whether a page receives a raw visit. Use one change at a time and keep the source tag/creative code stable. If Go arrivals are zero or unknown, stop: that is a measurement-readiness problem, not evidence that the landing page failed.

**Stage 3 — offer.** Only after a measurable first-use path exists, compare an honest beta with free Pro access against the current free-tier/offer language. Do not promise outcomes or manufacture testimonials. Track activation, signup completion, and returning use at a proposed 7-day and 14-day check, using native/product definitions rather than vanity views. Retention should mean a user returning and performing the defined useful action, not a repeat video play.

Manual interviews are a separate proposed research activity, not a substitute for analytics. Invite a small number of consenting viewers or known users to a short, voluntary conversation. Ask what job they thought the clip addressed, what they expected after tapping, what stopped first use, and what information they would not want to share. Do not recruit or target people based on medication, diagnosis, injury, or other sensitive health attributes. Record consent, avoid collecting unnecessary health details, and treat the answers as qualitative hypotheses.
